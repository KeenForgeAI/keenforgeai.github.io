Foundation models can't label what they've never seen

GPT-4V scores 22% on industrial defect detection. The factory needs 98%+.

This is not a tuning problem — it is structural. General and world models are trained on the common world: people, cars, animals, everyday objects. A hairline crease on a steel strip, a rare cell morphology, a specific crop blight, a particular security anomaly — those are out-of-distribution, and the model's recall collapses exactly where the stakes are highest.

So experts still have to annotate. A QC engineer, a radiologist, an agronomist — they can tell a defect from a shadow in a fraction of a second. What they could not do was turn that judgement into a working model, because that required writing code and training neural networks.

KeenForge closes that gap. The expert keeps doing what only they can do — judging the images. The tool does everything an image-algorithm engineer would normally do around them.

SAI: AI Must Embrace Specialization via Superhuman Adaptable Intelligence — Goldfeder, Wyder, LeCun & Shwartz-Ziv (2025) — argues the future of AI is not one general model, but thousands of specialised superhuman systems. That is the thesis KeenForge is built on.

Why "let the big model label it" fails on niche data

Manual labelling
Every image drawn by hand, forever. No feedback, no acceleration.

Niche data is slow and expensive.
Big-model auto-labelling
Works well on common objects. On rare specialist samples the model has barely seen, it guesses — and you spend more time fixing wrong boxes than drawing them.

Unreliable exactly where it matters.
KeenForge
You label a small seed set. The model trains on your domain, pre-labels the next batch, you correct it. Each round it needs less from you.

The model learns your expertise — then does the work.

A general model will never become an expert in your domain by itself — your defect, your tissue, your crop exists in the long tail it was never trained on. But a model can learn it from a surprisingly small number of expert-corrected examples. That is what the loop exploits: your corrections are the training signal, and the model's own mistakes are what tells it where to look next.

Built for the people who own the judgement

  • Quality & manufacturing engineers — steel, PCB, casting, textile, welding. Surface defects that no general detector has ever seen enough of.
  • Clinicians & pathologists — cell morphology, histology, radiology. Rare presentations, high cost of a wrong label.
  • Agronomists & ecologists — crop disease, pests, species monitoring. Long-tail classes, very few public images.
  • Security & safety teams — domain-specific anomalies, PPE compliance, site-specific risks.

We also curate public niche datasets

Public niche datasets are often large but noisy: loose boxes, missed objects, mislabels, duplicated images, class names that don't even match. Training on them caps your ceiling before you start.

We take these datasets, re-annotate and clean them with KeenForge, document every change, and publish them openly on Hugging Face and ModelScope — so anyone can start from a clean baseline instead of re-discovering the same defects.

Come build it with us

KeenForge is open source and community-driven. The goal is bigger than one tool: to distil what an image-algorithm engineer does, so that the people who actually understand the data can build the models. There is a lot still to do.

  • Use it and tell us what breaks — bug reports and feature requests shape the roadmap more than anything else. Open an issue.
  • Contribute code — the tool is MIT licensed. PRs, docs, tests and translations are all welcome.
  • Contribute a dataset — re-annotated a niche dataset with KeenForge? We will help you clean, document and publish it.
  • Spread the word — star the repo, share your workflow, point a domain expert at it.
